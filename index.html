National trend

How did non-fatal hospitalised road traffic injury counts change from 2011 to 2021, and how did the pattern in 2019–2021 compare with earlier years?

Overall Trend

Over the decade from 2011 to 2021, the absolute count of hospitalised road traffic injuries in Australia rose from 34,033 to 39,505—a net increase of 16%.

Recording Changes

It is critical to note two major administrative changes: Victoria in 2012 and New South Wales in 2017 updated their admission criteria. These administrative shifts created reporting "breaks" (indicated by the dashed yellow lines).

COVID-19 Dip and Rebound

Between 2019 and 2021, we observe a distinct COVID-19 pandemic dip (37,966 cases in 2020), followed by a rebound to 39,505 in 2021, just below the 2019 level of 39,866. The data cannot show why.

Conclusion

What the data tells us

Four findings from the 2011–2021 BITRE hospitalised injury data. Each one links back to the chart it comes from.

Q1 · National trend +16% Hospitalisations rose from 34,033 to 39,505. They dipped to 37,966 in 2020, then rebounded. Part of the rise reflects recording changes in Victoria (2012) and NSW (2017). Q2 · Road users +2,772 Pedal cyclists rose the most of any road user group, accounting for 50.7% of the net increase of 5,472. Q3 · States and territories 28.2% Victoria had the largest share in 2021, ahead of Queensland (26.4%) and New South Wales (22.8%). These are shares of counts, not rates. Q4 · Age, sex and road user 4,036 Car drivers aged 40–64 were the largest age and road user group in 2021. Males made up 65% of records with a recorded sex.

Counts describe how many people were hospitalised. They do not show risk, because they are not adjusted for population or distance travelled.

About the data

Where the numbers come from, and what they can’t tell you

Source and method

Bureau of Infrastructure and Transport Research Economics (BITRE), Hospitalised Injury data, covering 2011 to 2021. We counted non-fatal hospitalisations caused by road traffic crashes.

The raw files were cleaned and aggregated in KNIME, exported as the CSV files listed below, and drawn in the browser with D3.js. Nothing is estimated or modelled.

Limits to keep in mind
  • Counts, not rates. Larger states and larger age groups have more people, so bigger counts do not mean higher risk.
  • Recording changes. Victoria (2012) and New South Wales (2017) changed how admissions are recorded, so trends across those years are not strictly comparable. They are marked with a dagger (†) on the state map.
  • Patterns, not causes. The 2020 dip coincides with COVID-19 restrictions, but the data cannot show why.
  • Missing values. Records without a recorded age are left out of the age axis, and the very small group with indeterminate or missing sex appears only under “All” in the age and sex chart. Both are still included in the totals.
Glossary
Hospitalisation
One recorded hospital admission after a road traffic injury. Deaths are not counted.
Road user
The injured person’s role on the road, for example car driver, pedal cyclist or pedestrian.
Share
A group’s percentage of the national total in the same year.
Percentage points (pp)
The difference between two shares. A share moving from 20% to 23% is +3 pp.
Net change
The total in the end year minus the total in the start year.
