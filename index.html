National trend

How did non-fatal hospitalised road traffic injury counts change from 2011 to 2021, and how did the pattern in 2019–2021 compare with earlier years?

National annual hospitalisations (counts). Hover over or tap the markers (or drag a finger along the chart) to see year-over-year changes.

Overall Trend

Over the decade from 2011 to 2021, the absolute count of hospitalised road traffic injuries in Australia rose from 34,033 to 39,505—a net increase of 16%.

Recording Changes

It is critical to note two major administrative changes: Victoria in 2012 and New South Wales in 2017 updated their admission criteria. These administrative shifts created reporting "breaks" (indicated by the dashed yellow lines).

COVID-19 Dip and Rebound

Between 2019 and 2021, we observe a distinct COVID-19 pandemic dip (37,966 cases in 2020), followed by a rebound to 39,505 in 2021, just below the 2019 level of 39,866. The data cannot show why.

Conclusion

What the data tells us

Five findings from the 2011–2021 BITRE hospitalised injury data. Each one links back to the chart it comes from.

Q1 · National trend +16% Hospitalisations rose from 34,033 to 39,505. They dipped to 37,966 in 2020, then rebounded. Recording changes in Victoria (2012) and NSW (2017) affect comparisons across years. Q2 · Road users +2,772 Pedal cyclists rose the most of any road user group, accounting for 50.7% of the net increase of 5,472. Q3 · States and territories 28.2% Victoria had the largest share in 2021, ahead of Queensland (26.4%) and New South Wales (22.8%). These are shares of counts, not rates. Q4 · Age, sex and road user 4,036 Car drivers aged 40–64 were the largest age and road user group in 2021. Males made up 65% of records with a recorded sex. Q5 · First Nations peoples 30.90% In 2021, people aged 17–25 accounted for 30.90% of First Nations hospitalisations, compared with 19.66% for non-Indigenous people. These are within-group percentages.

Counts describe recorded hospitalisations. They do not directly measure individual injury risk because they are not adjusted for population size or exposure.

About the data

Where the numbers come from, and what they can’t tell you

Source and method

Bureau of Infrastructure and Transport Research Economics (BITRE), Hospitalised Injury data , covering 2011 to 2021. We counted non-fatal hospitalisations caused by road traffic crashes.

The raw files were cleaned and aggregated in KNIME, exported as CSV files, and drawn in the browser with D3.js.

Limits to keep in mind
  • Counts, not rates. Bigger counts do not necessarily mean higher individual risk.
  • Recording changes. Victoria (2012) and New South Wales (2017) changed admission reporting practices.
  • Patterns, not causes. The 2020 decrease coincides with the COVID-19 pandemic, but this dataset alone cannot explain why.
  • Missing values. Unpublished or incomplete First Nations categories were excluded during KNIME processing.
Glossary
Hospitalisation
One recorded hospital admission after a road traffic injury. Fatal cases are excluded.
Road user
The injured person's role on the road, such as car driver, motorcyclist, cyclist or pedestrian.
Share
A category's percentage of its relevant total.
Percentage points (pp)
The difference between two percentages.
Net change
Total in the end year minus total in the start year.
