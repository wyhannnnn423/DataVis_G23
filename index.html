National trend

How did non-fatal hospitalised road traffic injury counts change from 2011 to 2021, and how did the pattern in 2019–2021 compare with earlier years?

Overall Trend

Over the decade from 2011 to 2021, the absolute count of hospitalised road traffic injuries in Australia rose from 34,033 to 39,505—a net increase of 16%.

Recording Changes

It is critical to note two major administrative changes: Victoria in 2012 and New South Wales in 2017 updated their admission criteria. These administrative shifts created reporting "breaks" (indicated by the dashed yellow lines).

COVID-19 Dip and Rebound

Between 2019 and 2021, we observe a distinct COVID-19 pandemic dip (37,966 cases in 2020), followed by a rebound to 39,505 in 2021, just below the 2019 level of 39,866. The data cannot show why.

Conclusion

Summary of Key Road Safety Insights

Our analysis of the 2011–2021 BITRE hospitalisation data reveals three crucial dimensions of road traffic injuries in Australia:

  • Rising Trend: Total hospitalised injuries rose by 16% over the decade (34,033 to 39,505), although part of the movement reflects changes in how Victoria and New South Wales record admissions.
  • Pedal Cyclists: Pedal cyclists recorded the largest rise of any road user group (+2,772), accounting for 50.7% of the net increase.
  • Geographic Concentration: Victoria, Queensland, and New South Wales dominate national totals, with shifting relative shares heavily influenced by changes in admission criteria.
